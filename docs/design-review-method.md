# Design review method — observation to testable revision

[Return to showcase](../index.html) · [Original-work gallery](../index.html#archive) · [Case study](case-study.md)

The method below is distilled from the private PowerPortals design-process archive. Example instructions are paraphrased teaching patterns, **not purported verbatim conversation transcripts**.

## The review contract

1. **Observe.** Identify a frame, scene or specific time range. Describe only what can be seen or heard.
2. **Diagnose the communication cost.** Is the viewer reading too much, tracking too many objects, or inferring a missing step?
3. **Specify a bounded change.** Name the object, start condition, destination, pace, hold, alignment or text replacement.
4. **Preserve proof.** Keep correctly demonstrated relationships and clearly distinguish synthetic visuals from live product behavior.
5. **Review the exported result.** Judge motion and sound at playback speed. A still image cannot verify timing, perceived smoothness or audio quality.
6. **Document the trade-off.** Record if a simplification obscures detail, reduces disclosure time or changes audience scope.

## Example feedback contracts

### Visual hierarchy

**Observation:** Interface cards consume attention before the headline communicates its value.

**Change request:** Replace the card stack with a short headline and one central visual motif. Hold the completed headline before advancing.

**Acceptance check:** The scene has a dominant reading order and no simultaneous competing focal elements.

### Automation storytelling

**Observation:** A configuration view shows structure but not event order.

**Change request:** Start with the triggering action, reveal connected actions progressively and widen only as the chain grows.

**Acceptance check:** Viewers can identify the trigger and distinguish its downstream effects without inspecting configuration text. This check describes desired review behavior; no viewer study was conducted.

### Motion continuity

**Observation:** The animation technically connects two scenes but adds a secondary, distracting travel path.

**Change request:** Use a direct object-to-object transformation with a specified endpoint and no extra orbit.

**Acceptance check:** The focal object lands in the target position without a detour during normal-speed playback.

### Product language

**Observation:** A heading names a feature but does not explain why it matters.

**Change request:** Replace it with a recognizable question and a short payoff; retain the visual demonstration.

**Acceptance check:** The headline and action express the same problem-solution relationship without unsupported numerical claims.

## Practical checks

| Quality dimension | Inspect | Avoid overstating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition | Hierarchy, negative space, type baselines | “Better conversion” without data |
| Timing | Onset, hold, scene transitions | Motion quality from static frames alone |
| Product clarity | Trigger-action order and visible relationships | Live execution from an illustration |
| Source accuracy | Numbers, draft labels, data origin | Universal connectivity claims |
| Audio | Mix separation, source identity, perceived effect | Diagnosing extra sound layers from intuition |
| Evidence | Original exports, notes, manifest, limitations | Treating retrospective analysis as a contemporaneous transcript |

## Why preserve alternatives?

An earlier treatment may be appropriate for a different audience or information density. A useful review portfolio explains **why one version was selected** and why another was retained as an outtake, correction or valid alternative.

This methodology is presented for educational evaluation and is not a formal accreditation or independent benchmark.
