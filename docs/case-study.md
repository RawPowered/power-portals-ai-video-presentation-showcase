# Case study — turning iterative design feedback into a directed commercial

[Return to showcase](../index.html) · [Review method](design-review-method.md)

## The brief

PowerPortals was presented as a connected portal/workflow concept for solar and construction-oriented operations. The initial creative challenge was broad: explain project and deal management, materials, role-based tasks, automation, and other interconnected operations without making a commercial feel like an interface tutorial.

The private archive's opening work focused on functionality and capture evidence; the subsequent creative work shifted toward succinct, television-style presentation.

## Role and collaboration

**Creative direction and review:** Joseph Edwards / RawPowered — goals, audience, iterative art direction, critique, selected footage direction, final edit preferences and owner-provided soundtrack.

**AI-assisted implementation and documentation:** animated content, typography/layout implementation, motion/audio processing, versioned rendering, verification and retrospective case-study assembly.

The private repository documents 35 numbered stage folders, 24 video exports and 17 annotated critiques. These counts are archival metadata; they do not imply 35 independently completed videos, 17 tests with users, or measured effectiveness.

## Decision 01 — move beyond boxed software layouts

**Problem:** A TV-style pitch was still dominated by boxed areas and excessive on-screen structure.

**Design instruction:** Use large free-standing typography, deliberate negative space and one clear orange focal accent. Keep each concept concise enough to parse during playback.

**Outcome recorded:** A later commercial-oriented direction reduced the boxed presentation vocabulary. The earlier approach remained a valid direction study, not a universally bad layout choice.

## Decision 02 — animate causal order

**Problem:** A configuration panel could show that an automation was set up, but did not make the triggered sequence clear to a casual viewer.

**Design instruction:** Show a trigger first. Introduce downstream actions in logical order, and broaden the composition as more branches appear.

**Outcome recorded:** The presentation used a growing workflow illustration. It is evidence of explanatory storytelling, **not** proof that a new live automation execution occurred.

## Decision 03 — optimize motion by eliminating travel

**Problem:** A transition successfully matched its endpoints but still detoured through a tiny, moving dot before resolving into the final slash.

**Design instruction:** Contract directly into the slash, resolve at its final position and stop unnecessary perspective drift.

**Outcome recorded:** A subsequent version selected the more direct path. This illustrates the difference between technically continuous animation and perceptually efficient motion.

## Decision 04 — rewrite features as audience payoffs

**Problem:** Feature headings described capabilities rather than a buyer's day-to-day problems.

**Design instruction:** Retain the demonstrated action but pair it with an approachable question and a brief, concrete payoff. One archived example revised generic ownership language toward “Who owns this?” and “Every task. An owner.”

**Outcome recorded:** A benefit-led version was produced as a separate editorial choice, preserving the previous direction as an alternative rather than retroactively declaring it a failure.

## Other documented production choices

The private critique record also addresses typography baselines, visual continuity between tasks and handoffs, regional relevance, industry adaptability, sound-source provenance, and closing disclosure. The selected final private export is recorded as **114 seconds, 1920 × 1080, 60 fps**, with a five-second compact footer. Its shortened disclosure is an acknowledged readability trade-off, not evidence of better legal or comprehension outcomes.

## Outcomes and evidence limits

What the archive supports: iterative human critique, documented revision intent, exported visual changes, selected source-provenance checks, timing and frame validation, and explicit distinctions between actual captures and illustrated examples.

What it does **not** support: measured conversion or sales lift, proven cross-platform API compatibility, independent formal evaluation of design quality, or proof that every displayed simulated workflow ran in production.

**Public evidence note:** The homepage now includes [four genuine annotated critique boards and four silent original motion excerpts](../index.html#archive) with [source hashes](../assets/archive/README.md). The four separate interactive concept studies remain new abstractions, **not** original footage.

## Publication approach

Four specific critique boards and four silent animations are included as source examples; the complete commercial and soundtrack, raw captures, and the remainder of the archive stay private. The portfolio demonstrates identifying communication problems, giving bounded directions, reviewing changes and documenting limits.
